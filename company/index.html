Why we built NeoMind

Most companies don’t have a knowledge problem because they lack knowledge. They have a knowledge problem because it’s everywhere and nowhere: in a folder someone named badly, in an email thread three people were on, in the memory of a colleague who’s on leave. The same questions get asked every week. Leads arrive after hours and go cold. And when someone resigns, years of hard-won context leaves with them — you don’t even know what you lost until you need it.

We didn’t think the answer was another chatbot bolted onto a website. The answer was to treat AI the way you treat people: give it your knowledge, give it a job, and hold it to a standard. That meant building a single Brain — one place your organisation’s knowledge lives — and a set of digital employees that all draw from it. Change a fact once in the Brain, and every employee is instantly up to date.

Our mission

Turn the knowledge a business already has into employees it can rely on — grounded in its own information, taking real actions, and measurable like any member of staff.

That last word matters. An AI you can’t measure is an AI you can’t trust with real work. So every NeoMind employee can be given a business objective and weighted KPIs, and every conversation is scored against that rubric — guardrail violations score negative, so the number can’t be gamed. The result is an AI you manage with evidence, not hope.

How we think about the work

We hold a clear bright line: a NeoMind employee does the routine work brilliantly and at volume — answering, capturing, booking, deflecting — but it never signs, commits, or makes a binding promise on your behalf. When a conversation reaches a judgement call, the right move is to escalate to a human, and the scoring rewards exactly that. We think this honesty is what makes a measurable AI employee safe to actually deploy.

And your knowledge stays yours. NeoMind learns from the sources you give it to serve you; it isn’t a way for your data to train someone else’s model. Trust is the product.
